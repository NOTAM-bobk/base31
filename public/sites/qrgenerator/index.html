Tips for codes that scan first time

A QR code fails for boring reasons far more often than technical ones. These few rules cover almost every case.

Keep the contrast high

Dark modules on a light background is the reliable combination. Inverted codes fail on many older scanner apps.

Leave the quiet zone alone

The empty border is part of the code, not padding. Keep at least four modules of space on every side.

Print big enough

Around two centimetres square is the practical minimum; match the size to the scanning distance, roughly ten to one.

Test before you print

Scan the screen with two or three different phones. On a proof, scan the printed copy in the lighting it will live in.

Frequently asked questions

Are the QR codes free to use?
Yes. Every QR code generated here is free, has no expiry and never points through a redirect, so it keeps working as long as the destination does. There is no account and no watermark.
Do the QR codes expire or track scans?
No. The code encodes your exact content — a URL, Wi-Fi credentials, a contact card — with no shortener or tracking URL in between, and nothing is stored on a server. Scan counts therefore do not exist, which is usually what you want for print.
Which error correction level should I choose?
Higher levels survive more damage but make the pattern denser. Medium (M) is the everyday default, Quartile (Q) suits print, and High (H) is the right choice when you overlay a logo. The tool boosts the level automatically when you add one.
Why does my printed QR code not scan?
Almost always contrast or size. Keep the foreground dark and the background light, leave the quiet zone border intact, and print at least two centimetres square. Avoid putting a QR code on a curved, glossy or moving surface.
Is my data uploaded anywhere?
No. The QR code is computed and rendered in your browser. Your text, Wi-Fi password and contact details never leave the device, and the optional recent list is stored only in your own browser storage.
Can I use the code commercially?
Yes. There is no license fee or attribution requirement on codes you generate, though QR Code itself is a registered trademark of DENSO WAVE. Print the file you downloaded and use it on packaging, posters or business cards.
